Introduction to IT Systems

Core study modules and high-priority exam questions.

⚡ Programming, Algorithms & Flowcharts

  • High Priority Define Algorithm and list its main advantages and uses.
  • Why is an algorithm used before any programming?
  • High Priority Draw a Flowchart and write an Algorithm to find the highest/largest number among three given numbers.
  • High Priority Write an Algorithm and Draw a Flowchart to convert temperature from Celsius to Fahrenheit.
  • High Priority State the advantages of using flowcharts and describe the various symbols used in them.
  • Draw a flowchart to calculate the factorial of an integer.
  • Distinguish between loop and branching statements.
  • Define source code and executable code.

⚙️ Operating Systems

  • High Priority What is an Operating System? Describe its basic functions, services, and general architecture.
  • High Priority Define and distinguish between various types of Operating Systems (Batch Processing, Real-time, Multiprocessing, Time-sharing, and Multiprogramming).
  • Explain the advantages of multi-user and multitasking operating systems.
  • What is Booting and what is a bootstrap program?
  • What are CPU-bound and I/O-bound jobs?
  • Explain client-server architecture.

🎛️ Digital Logic & Number Systems

  • High Priority Define Universal Logic Gates and explain why they are termed as "universal."
  • High Priority State and prove (or explain) De-Morgan’s Law/Theorem in Boolean algebra.
  • High Priority Explain Gray code, its importance, and perform binary-to-gray or decimal-to-gray conversions.
  • High Priority Explain 1's and 2's complement representation and perform arithmetic operations using the 2's complement method.
  • High Priority Represent numbers using specific codes such as 2-4-2-1 BCD, 4-2-2-1, 8-4-2-1, and Excess-3.
  • Prove Boolean expressions or identities, such as ABC' + AB′C + A′BC + ABC = C.
  • Draw logic diagrams for gates like EX-OR and EX-NOR and discuss their operations.
  • Perform general number system conversions (e.g., Decimal to Octal, Binary to Decimal, Decimal to Hexadecimal).
  • What are ASCII and EBCDIC codes?
  • Simplify Boolean expressions such as (X+Y)(X+Y')(X'+Z).

🌐 Web Technologies (HTML, CSS, JS)

  • High Priority What is CSS and in how many ways can it be applied or integrated into an HTML document?
  • High Priority Explain the utility and advantages of using JavaScript.
  • High Priority Write HTML code to create a table for specific sets of data.
  • Write down the various formatting tags used in HTML.
  • Differentiate between ordered and unordered lists in HTML.
  • Explain the differences between the <picture> tag and <img> tag.
  • How can an image be inserted into a web page using HTML tags?
  • In how many ways can JavaScript code be involved or included in an HTML file?
  • What are the key differences between Java and JavaScript?
  • What is the difference between static and dynamic web pages?
  • Write a JavaScript program for specific tasks (e.g., calculating the sum of n natural numbers).
  • What is the difference between the == and === operators in JavaScript?
  • Define cascading and provide the proper syntax for introducing a Style Sheet in HTML.
  • Do all HTML tags require an end tag?

🛡️ Computer Security & Internet

  • High Priority Define Computer Hacking and Ethical Hacking, including the advantages of the latter.
  • High Priority What is a Denial of Service (DoS) attack and what are the security measures against it?
  • High Priority Define malicious software like viruses and worms, and explain how they work.
  • High Priority Explain the importance of search engines and the differences between a web browser and a search engine.
  • Why is computer security important and how can you protect a personal computer from being hacked?
  • What is cyber stalking?
  • What are Spam and Phishing?
  • Why have Firewalls become so important today?
  • Write the full forms of e-mail and www.
  • Write down the objectives of Digital India.

💾 Hardware, Memory & Software Basics

  • High Priority Explain the different types of software (System vs. Application) and provide examples.
  • Differentiate between various types of memory: Primary vs. Secondary, SRAM vs. DRAM, and HDD vs. SSD.
  • Define ROM, PROM, EPROM, and EEPROM.
  • Draw the block diagram of a digital computer and specify all its parts.
  • What is meant by cache memory?
  • What are the characteristics of computers?
  • What is the difference between a compiler and an interpreter?
  • Write about the utility of a scripting language.

Fundamentals of Electrical & Electronics Engineering (FEEE)

Core study modules, circuit theorems, and recurring exam questions.

⚡ Chapter 1: DC Circuits & Network Theorems

  • High Priority Define ideal current source and ideal voltage source with their respective symbols.
  • High Priority Explain the principle of Source Transformation and use it to calculate the current passing through a specific resistor.
  • Write down the unit of resistivity and name the primary factors affecting a conductor's resistance.
  • Under what specific conditions does the electrical resistance of a conductor increase?
  • Differentiate between active and passive electronic components, providing relevant examples of each.
  • Define capacitance, state its unit, and derive the formula for equivalent capacitance in parallel connections.
  • Define the time constant of a series R-C circuit and state the frequency of a standard D.C. signal.

🧲 Chapter 2: Electromagnetism & Magnetic Circuits

  • High Priority State Faraday’s Laws of Electromagnetic Induction in detail.
  • High Priority State Fleming’s Left-Hand Rule and list its primary practical applications.
  • High Priority Define and contrast the concepts of Self-Inductance and Mutual-Inductance.
  • State Lenz’s Law and Fleming’s Right-Hand Rule.
  • Define the following terms: Magnetic Flux Density, MMF, Permeability, Reluctance, and Hysteresis loss.
  • Draw an explicit comparison between Magnetic and Electric circuits, highlighting their structural similarities.

🔌 Chapters 3 & 4: AC Circuits & Three-Phase Systems

  • High Priority What is power factor? State its exact value for a purely resistive circuit.
  • High Priority Show mathematically that current leads the applied voltage by 90 degrees in a purely capacitive circuit.
  • High Priority Draw structural diagrams for both STAR and DELTA connections, stating their voltage and current relationships.
  • High Priority Define active power, reactive power, and the term "form factor."
  • Define RMS value, Frequency, and Periodic vs. Non-periodic waveforms.
  • Explain the behavior of current in a series RLC circuit under the condition of electrical resonance.

📟 Chapters 5 & 6: Transformers & Electrical Machines

  • High Priority Explain why electrical transformer ratings are strictly designated in kVA instead of kW.
  • High Priority Identify which core quantity (e.g., Frequency) remains absolutely constant during voltage transformation.
  • Explain the underlying working principle of a standard two-winding transformer and the purpose of core laminations.
  • Contrast power transfer mechanics in auto-transformers (conduction vs. induction).
  • Name the four main constructional parts of a DC motor and list common applications for DC series motors in traction systems.

🔬 Chapter 7: Semiconductor Devices & Rectifiers

  • High Priority Explain and differentiate the energy level band diagrams of an insulator, conductor, and semiconductor.
  • High Priority Define the term Doping and state common material examples used to create P-type and N-type semiconductors.
  • High Priority Draw the schematic symbols for both PNP and NPN transistors using proper terminal labeling.
  • Differentiate between Intrinsic and Extrinsic semiconductors.
  • Discuss forward and reverse bias characteristics of a standard P-N junction diode.
  • Explain why standard Field Effect Transistors (FETs) are classified as voltage-controlled devices.

🎛️ Chapters 8 & 9: Op-Amps & Digital Electronics

  • High Priority Draw the pin diagram of an IC 741 Op-Amp and describe the exact function assigned to each pin.
  • High Priority Explain the concept of a Virtual Ground in Operational Amplifiers.
  • High Priority Draw the circuit diagram of an inverting amplifier configuration and derive the expression for its output voltage.
  • High Priority Define Universal Logic Gates, provide examples, and explain what happens to a NAND gate output when both inputs are high.
  • High Priority State and explain De-Morgan’s Theorems using corresponding logic truth tables.
  • List the characteristics of an ideal Op-Amp regarding its gain, input resistance, and output resistance.
  • Define sequential logic circuits, name common types of Flip-Flops, and state the full forms of SOP, POS, and TTL.

Engineering Mechanics

Core theoretical frameworks, statutory definition principles, and recurring numerical problems.

⚖️ Statics & Systems of Forces

  • High Priority State and explain Lami’s Theorem for coplanar concurrent force systems.
  • High Priority State the general conditions required for the absolute equilibrium of coplanar forces.
  • High Priority State and explain Varignon’s Principle (Theorem of Moments).
  • Explain the Principle of Transmissibility of forces.
  • Explain the utility of Bow’s Notation in resolving structural frameworks.
  • Numerical Force System Resultant: Four forces act at a concurrent point:
    • 20N inclined at 30° north of east
    • 25N acting towards north
    • 30N acting towards north of west
    • 35N inclined at 40° south of west.
    Find the exact magnitude and directional orientation of the resultant force.

📐 Center of Gravity & Centroids

  • High Priority Define and explicitly differentiate between the Center of Gravity (C.G.) and a Centroid.
  • Define the structural concept of Center of Gravity as the explicit point through which the net weight of a body acts.
  • Numerical I-Section Centroid: Calculate the spatial centroid of an unsymmetrical or symmetrical I-section given the dimensional parameters of the flanges and web.
  • Numerical T-Section C.G.: Determine the exact position of the center of gravity from the bottom plane for a structural T-section measuring 100 mm × 150 mm × 50 mm.

🔩 Dynamics of Friction

  • High Priority Define the Coefficient of Friction and explain its relation to the Angle of Repose.
  • High Priority State Coulomb’s Laws of dry friction.
  • Define and illustrate the geometric boundaries of the Cone of Friction with a diagram.
  • What do you understand by the technical terms 'effort lost in friction' and 'frictional resistance' within engineering assemblies?

🏎️ Kinematics & Kinetics

  • High Priority State Newton’s Laws of Motion, focusing explicitly on the physical definition of the First Law (Law of Inertia).
  • State and explain the algebraic relationship connecting linear velocity to angular velocity (v = ωr).
  • Numerical Uniform Acceleration: A body moving with uniform acceleration covers 20 m in the 4th second and 30 m in the 8th second. Compute its initial velocity and acceleration.
  • Numerical Train Retardation: A train traveling at a constant velocity of 80 km/h is brought to a stop by a constant brake retardation of 0.74 m/s². Calculate the elapsed time required to completely stop.
  • Numerical Vertical Projectile: A ball is projected vertically upwards from ground level with an initial velocity of 2 m/s. Find the greatest height attained by the ball.

🏋️ Work, Power, Energy & Lifting Machines

  • High Priority Define Work, Power, and Energy, specifying their correct S.I. units of measurement.
  • High Priority Differentiate between a reversible lifting machine and a non-reversible (self-locking) lifting machine.
  • High Priority Define Mechanical Advantage (MA) and Velocity Ratio (VR), and show how their ratio defines the absolute efficiency of a lifting machine.
  • What is meant by the "law of a machine"? Outline the distinct structural advantages of using a lifting machine.
  • Show using neat schematic layouts: a Uniformly Distributed Load (UDL) and a Uniformly Varying Load (UVL) acting on a structural member.
  • Explain why the mechanical efficiency of an ideal machine is evaluated as exactly 100%.

Applied Physics-II

Core experimental frameworks, wave mechanics, electrostatics, and modern semiconductor principles.

🔊 Oscillations, Waves & Acoustics

  • Repeating State the fundamental characteristics of Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM).
  • Repeating Define and distinguish between Free, Damped, and Forced vibrations.
  • Repeating What is an ultrasonic wave? Outline its unique physical properties and major applications.
  • Numerical Wave Beats: Two wave sources with frequencies of 400 Hz and 405 Hz interfere. Calculate the resulting beat frequency, and prove that beats per second equal the frequency difference.
  • Numerical SHM Parameters: A particle vibrates according to the equation: y = 5 sin [π/5(10t + x)]. Compute its absolute frequency and amplitude.
  • What are the primary structural requirements and corrective measures needed to control reverberation in an auditorium?
  • Differentiate clearly between transverse and longitudinal wave motion. In which medium can a transverse wave not propagate?

👁️ Optics & Fiber Transmission

  • Repeating Define the following terms: Critical Angle, Refractive Index, Optical Center, and 1 Dioptre.
  • Repeating State the mandatory boundary conditions required for Total Internal Reflection (TIR) to occur.
  • Repeating Explain what happens to the net focal length of an optical lens when it is completely immersed in water.
  • Explain the physical mechanism of light propagation through an optical fiber. Why does the core possess a higher refractive index than the cladding?
  • Numerical Lens Combo: Two thin lenses with individual powers of +5D and -2.5D are placed in direct contact. Determine the focal length of the combination.
  • Draw clean ray diagrams illustrating:
    • A convex lens creating a real, inverted, and magnified image.
    • The structural layout and beam paths of a compound microscope.
  • Distinguish between interference fringes in Young's Double Slit Experiment (YDSE) and the diffraction pattern produced by a single slit.

⚡ Electrostatics & Circuit Laws

  • Repeating State Gauss’ Law in electrostatics and use it to explain the electric field inside a hollow charged sphere.
  • Repeating Define the S.I. unit of resistivity of a conductor, and explain the difference between resistance and specific resistance.
  • Repeating State Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law (KVL) and explain the balanced condition of a Wheatstone Bridge with a neat circuit schematic.
  • Repeating Define the Seebeck effect and distinguish it from the classic Joule effect.
  • Define Electric Potential and Electric Flux. Prove that the total energy stored in a parallel plate capacitor equals ½ CV².
  • Numerical Lamp Resistance: An electric lamp is rated 100W, 220V. Compute its running resistance.
  • Numerical Capacitor Network: Find the equivalent capacitance for a network consisting of a 6 μF and 4 μF capacitor in parallel, connected in series with an 8 μF capacitor.

🧲 Magnetism & Induction

  • Repeating State Lenz's Law and explain its direct relation to the absolute principle of conservation of energy.
  • Repeating Deduce the mathematical expression for the magnetic field at the exact center of a circular current-carrying coil.
  • Repeating State the Biot-Savart Law in its vector and mathematical forms.
  • Repeating How is a basic moving-coil galvanometer modified to function safely as an ammeter?
  • State Faraday's laws of electromagnetic induction. Why is an induced electromotive force often termed a "back emf"?
  • Distinguish between the structural profiles of Diamagnetic, Paramagnetic, and Ferromagnetic materials.
  • Numerical Induced EMF: Calculate the induced EMF in a 1mH inductor when the passing current drops uniformly from 5A to 3A in 10³¹ seconds.

🔬 Semiconductors, Lasers & Nanotechnology

  • Repeating Differentiate between insulators, semiconductors, and conductors by sketching their corresponding energy band diagrams.
  • Repeating State the structural differences between P-type and N-type semiconductors, including the doping profiles of transistor layers (Emitter, Base, Collector).
  • Repeating What does the acronym LASER stand for? State its core characteristics, define stimulated emission, and outline methods for achieving population inversion.
  • Repeating Draw the forward and reverse V-I characteristic curves for a P-N junction diode, and explain its utility for rectification (AC to DC conversion).
  • Repeating Explain what a solar cell is and discuss why it serves as an ideal energy option for remote rural communities.
  • Draw the detailed energy level diagram of a Helium-Neon (He-Ne) Laser.
  • What is the full form of a CNT in nanotechnology? List three practical uses of nanotechnology in engineering fields.
